These maps show how big those zones are, who lives in them, and how much they change depending on which offices you count.

The big picture

One office for every zone

Each coloured zone belongs to one office. Blue are lower-tier offices (a town or township inside a region), green are single-tier cities and townships that run everything themselves.

Zones are small where offices are close together, mostly across southern Ontario, and large where a single office covers a lot of land.

Zones versus borders

The tan and pink shapes are the original municipal boundaries. Offices sit in one town, so the zones don't follow the borders: in places a zone crosses into a neighbouring municipality because that office is simply closer.

About the numbers

  • Distances are straight-line, not driving distance, and are weighted by 2021 census population (dissemination-area centres).
  • Only land inside Ontario's lower- and single-tier municipal boundaries is mapped, which covers __COVERAGE__% of the province's population. Unincorporated areas are left blank.
  • Offices come from a hand-collected list; about __LOW_CONF__ of the __TOTAL__ have geocodes with confidence below 0.9 and may be a little off. __CHECKS__ possible data issues are listed in data/office_checks.csv.
